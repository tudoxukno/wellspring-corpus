## Known defect — the Kingdoms books are shown under the wrong names (recorded 23 September 2026)

`content/vul/` and `content/lxx/` (and `content/brenton/`) display **1 Samuel, 2 Samuel, 1 Kings, 2 Kings**. Neither
edition calls them that. The Septuagint has four books of **Kingdoms** (Βασιλειῶν Α΄–Δ΄); the Clementine Vulgate and
the Douay have **1–4 Kings**, where 1–2 Kings are Samuel and 3–4 Kings are what a Protestant Bible calls Kings.
Brenton's own English prints *"The First Book of the Kings (commonly called the First Book of Samuel)"*.

No book is missing — all four are held in both packages — but a Latin text under Protestant titles is the corpus
rule broken by the corpus itself, and it is the one thing Wellspring claims no competitor does. The OSIS ids
(`1Sam`, `1Kgs`) are machine keys and are correct as they stand; the fix is the **displayed** name, taken from each
edition's own title page rather than composed here, and shown with the familiar name beside it so a reader looking
for 1 Kings still finds it.

`content/geez/` does not have this problem and is the model: its Kings is labelled *2 Kings (Nagast)* and opens at
2 Kings 1:1, and its Apocalypse of Ezra is kept separate from 2 Esdras.
